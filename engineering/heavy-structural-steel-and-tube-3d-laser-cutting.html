1. The Four Fundamental Challenges in Heavy Profile Laser Cutting

Unlike standard flat sheet metal cutting where the workpiece rests on a flat slat table, structural steel profiles present non-linear geometry, residual manufacturing stresses, and significant gravitational deformation:

1. Gravitational Deflection (Sag)

A standard 12-meter HEA-400 beam can deflect over 6.5mm at its midpoint under gravity alone, throwing 3D laser focus off and creating collision hazards.

2. Thermal Cambering & Bowing

Continuous high-power laser slotting on one flange releases internal rolling stresses and creates temperature gradients, causing lateral curvature.

3. Variable Flange Thickness

Hot-rolled I-beams exhibit tapered flanges (typically 8%–14% slope), requiring dynamic focal point adjustments and variable gas pressure regulation.

4. Scrap & Tailing Waste

Traditional two-chuck machines leave 800mm–1200mm un-cut tailing scrap per beam. With structural steel at $1,100/ton, this creates tens of thousands in lost margin.

When cutting longitudinal bolt holes and flange notches in H-beams, ACTCNC's proprietary CAM software automatically simulates the localized heat-affected zone and splits the toolpath into opposing interleaved cutting segments. 4. Frequently Asked Questions (Engineering FAQ)

Q1: How does the 4-chuck system eliminate un-cut tailing scrap on 12-meter beams?
Traditional machines require chucks to maintain a safety distance from the cutting head, resulting in 800mm to 1200mm of untouched scrap. ACTCNC's system features two stationary and two mobile synchronized chucks. As the cutting head approaches the beam end, the rear chuck hands off the workpiece directly to the front chucks inside the cutting envelope, enabling the laser to cut clean through the absolute end of the raw material with 0mm scrap.
Q2: Can ACTCNC machines cut bolt holes and cope cuts in tapered-flange I-beams?
Yes. Using real-time non-contact capacitive height sensing integrated with dynamic 5-axis focal positioning, the laser head automatically tracks the tapered slope of the inner flange surface without collision, maintaining a constant 0.8mm nozzle stand-off height and consistent kerf geometry.
Q3: How does laser 3D weld beveling compare to robotic plasma cutting?
Fiber laser beveling provides a heat-affected zone (HAZ) under 0.3mm (compared to 2.5mm+ with plasma), surface roughness Ra < 12.5 μm, and precise root-face tolerances of ±0.15mm. This enables immediate robotic automated welding without manual grinding or de-slagging.
